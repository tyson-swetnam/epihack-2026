Wildlife & Vector-Borne Disease — Data Resources & Collection Programs

A working catalog of agencies and programs that collect or hold data relevant to wildlife and vector-borne disease surveillance in Arizona, organized by jurisdiction. Each entry includes top-level URLs, the program’s data products, and how it can plug into a participatory surveillance system and the DuckLake knowledge graph.

State of Arizona

Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS)

  • Website: https://www.azdhs.gov/
  • Vector-Borne & Zoonotic Diseases program: https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/vector-borne-zoonotic-diseases/
  • Mosquito-borne program: https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/mosquito-borne/
  • Role. Statewide public-health authority. Operates the Vector-Borne & Zoonotic Disease (VBZD) team that works with the Arizona Department of Agriculture and AZGFD on cross-sector animal disease events. Owns the Arizona State Public Health Laboratory.
  • Surveillance. West Nile virus, St. Louis encephalitis, dengue, Zika, hantavirus (spike of 6 cases in 2023 and 11 in 2024 with six deaths), plague, tularemia, rabies, Rocky Mountain spotted fever (active 200+ owned/stray dog seroprevalence project in Cochise, Santa Cruz, and Yuma counties). Uses syndromic surveillance for rapid identification of arboviral cases.
  • Documents. The Arizona One Health Toolkit (2019) is the formal multi-agency coordination playbook.

Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD)

  • Website: https://www.azgfd.com/
  • Wildlife Diseases page: https://www.azgfd.com/wildlife-conservation/wildlife-diseases-2/
  • Wildlife Conservation Strategy — Disease chapter: https://awcs.azgfd.com/conservation-challenges/disease-pathogens-and-parasites
  • Role. Wildlife data authority for Arizona. Investigates significant wildlife morbidity and mortality events.
  • Chronic Wasting Disease (CWD). Not yet detected in Arizona despite bordering three CWD-positive states. AZGFD has tested 25,000+ samples since 1998, collecting ~1,200–1,600 samples per year via check stations, hunt camps, taxidermists, game-meat processors, and hunter submissions.
  • HPAI / avian influenza. Active wild-bird surveillance.
  • Other programs. Plague die-off response in prairie-dog colonies, bighorn pneumonia, brucellosis, mange.
  • Public reporting. Public can report dead or sick wildlife to the Wildlife Health Program directly.

Arizona Department of Agriculture

  • Website: https://agriculture.az.gov/
  • Role. Animal-industry regulator. Coordinates with AZGFD and ADHS on cross-sector zoonoses (e.g. brucellosis in livestock, HPAI in domestic poultry interfaces).

Arizona One Health (multi-agency initiative)

  • Coordinating reference: Arizona One Health Toolkit (2019)
  • Partners include: ADHS, AZGFD, Arizona Department of Agriculture, USDA APHIS Wildlife Services, USDA APHIS Veterinary Services, AZVDL, CDC, University of Arizona.

Arizona counties

Maricopa County

  • Vector Control (MCESD): https://www.maricopa.gov/632/Vector-Control
  • Public dashboard / data: https://www.maricopa.gov/2476/Mosquito-Borne-Disease-Statistics
  • Public-facing site: https://fightthebitemaricopa.org/
  • Vector-borne & zoonotic reports: https://www.maricopa.gov/4236/Vector-borne-Zoonotic-Reports
  • Network. Over 800 vector traps county-wide, mosquito pools tested for WNV and SLE, with a published weekly Vector Index (VI) that estimates the proportion of infected mosquitoes per species per area. Data is updated weekly on Fridays.
  • Notable. Maricopa County had an “unprecedented” 2021 West Nile outbreak documented in CDC MMWR.

Pima County Health Department

  • Vector Control Program: https://www.pima.gov/2296/Vector-Control-Program
  • Mosquitoes: https://www.pima.gov/2100/Mosquitoes
  • Trapping window. May through November; focus on Culex tarsalis and Culex quinquefasciatus.
  • Additional program. Free Gila topminnow distribution program (a native fish) for biological mosquito control in approved water bodies such as golf-course ponds.
  • Hotline. 520-724-7908.

Other county programs

County health departments in Coconino, Yavapai, Yuma, Pinal, Mohave, Cochise, Santa Cruz, Apache, Navajo, La Paz, Greenlee, Graham, Gila each run their own surveillance to varying scales; most feed weekly summaries into ADHS during arbovirus season.

Tribal nations and tribal epidemiology centers

Arizona is home to 22 federally recognized tribal nations. Many run their own health, natural-resource, and veterinary programs that hold data not otherwise visible in state-level reports. Any wildlife / vector-borne participatory system must engage tribal partners as co-designers, not as data sources.

The 22 federally recognized tribes in Arizona

Tribal Epidemiology Centers (TECs)

Navajo Epidemiology Center (NEC)

  • Website: https://nec.navajo-nsn.gov/
  • Profile: https://tribalepicenters.org/navajo-epidemiology-center/
  • Established: 2005. Manages the Navajo Nation’s public-health information systems, runs the Navajo Nation Health Survey (NNHS — a Navajo-specific BRFSS adaptation), and investigates diseases and injuries of concern. Critical context for hantavirus surveillance — Sin Nombre virus was first characterized during the 1993 Four Corners outbreak.
  • 2025 agreement. Signed a Memorandum of Agreement with the New Mexico Department of Health for stronger cross-jurisdiction data sharing.

Inter Tribal Council of Arizona — Tribal Epidemiology Center (ITCA-TEC)

  • Website: https://itcaonline.com/programs/research-and-evaluation/epidemiology/
  • Profile: https://tribalepicenters.org/inter-tribal-council-of-arizona-tribal-epidemiology-center/
  • Role. Builds tribally-driven public-health and epidemiologic capacity across the Phoenix and Tucson IHS Areas. Based in Phoenix.

Tribal natural-resource and health departments (selected examples)

  • Navajo Nation Department of Fish and Wildlife — wildlife authority for the largest reservation in the U.S. (parts of AZ, NM, UT). Plague is endemic; rodent and flea die-offs are tracked locally.
  • Tohono O’odham Nation Health & Human Services: https://www.tonation-nsn.gov/health-human-services/ — serves ~28,000 members across 2.8 million acres in southwestern Arizona. Sells Indian Hospital (IHS) is the principal facility.
  • Hopi Tribe, Hualapai Tribe, Havasupai Tribe — northern AZ wildlife / vector contexts overlap with Coconino County’s hantavirus, plague, and RMSF surveillance.
  • White Mountain Apache Tribe and San Carlos Apache Tribe — large reservations with significant wildlife populations.
  • Pascua Yaqui Tribe (Tucson area) and Gila River Indian Community / Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community (Phoenix area) — urban-edge wildlife and vector exposure.

Indian Health Service (IHS)

  • Phoenix Area: https://www.ihs.gov/phoenix/ — covers AZ, NV, UT and serves ~180,000 American Indian / Alaska Native users.
  • Tucson Area: services include Sells Indian Hospital.

Federal agencies

Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)

  • One Health hub: https://www.cdc.gov/one-health/
  • National One Health Framework (2023, released by CDC, USDA, DOI): Federal Register notice · framework PDF
  • U.S. One Health Coordination Unit (OHCU) — brings together 27 federal agencies. Arizona has participated in the U.S. One Health Zoonotic Disease Prioritization (OHZDP) workshop process.

USDA APHIS Wildlife Services

  • National: https://www.aphis.usda.gov/wildlife-services
  • National Wildlife Disease Program — Surveillance and Emergency Response System (SERS): https://www.aphis.usda.gov/national-wildlife-programs/nwdp/sers
  • Avian Health in Wildlife (HPAI): https://www.aphis.usda.gov/national-wildlife-programs/nwdp/avian-health
  • Arizona program focus. Predation management, endangered species, invasive species, urban coyotes, and zoonotic diseases — especially plague and rabies.
  • Avian-influenza wild-bird detections: https://www.aphis.usda.gov/livestock-poultry-disease/avian/avian-influenza/hpai-detections/wild-birds

USDA APHIS Veterinary Services

  • National Animal Health Laboratory Network (NAHLN). AZVDL (see Academic section) is a member.

U.S. Geological Survey — National Wildlife Health Center (USGS NWHC)

  • Center: https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc
  • Data & Tools: https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc/data
  • WHISPers (Wildlife Health Information Sharing Partnership — event reporting system): https://whispers.usgs.gov/ — web repository of historic and current wildlife mortality and morbidity events reported by partners nationwide; also the request portal for NWHC diagnostic services.
  • National Wildlife Disease Database: https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc/science/national-wildlife-disease-database-partner-us-protect-wildlife-agriculture-pet
  • HPAI distribution in North America: https://www.usgs.gov/centers/nwhc/science/distribution-highly-pathogenic-avian-influenza-north-america-20212022
  • Contact: whispers@usgs.gov

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (USFWS)

  • Arizona Ecological Services Field Office: https://www.fws.gov/office/arizona-ecological-services
  • Arizona Fish and Wildlife Conservation Office: https://www.fws.gov/office/arizona-fish-and-wildlife-conservation
  • Avian Influenza program: https://www.fws.gov/avian-influenza
  • Footprint. Administers ~1.8 million acres in Arizona via National Wildlife Refuges and National Fish Hatcheries.
  • American Rescue Plan Act Zoonotic Disease Grant Program: https://www.fws.gov/project/american-rescue-plan-act-zoonotic-disease-grant-program — Arizona received $134,320 for “Expanding Zoonotic Disease Surveillance and Response in Arizona through Interagency Coordination and Resource Development”.

National Park Service (NPS)

  • Grand Canyon — Zoonotic Diseases in Wildlife: https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/zoonotic_diseases.htm
  • Coconino County HHS partners with NPS at Grand Canyon for hantavirus, plague, West Nile virus, and rabies surveillance. Recent confirmed-rabies bat collected near Kanab Creek Canyon.
  • Saguaro National Park — investigated wildlife mortality and unusual behavior (foxes; potential rabies link) in collaboration with Pima County and ADHS.

Bureau of Land Management (BLM)

  • Assessment, Inventory, and Monitoring (AIM): https://www.blm.gov/aim
  • TerrADat (national terrestrial AIM database hub): https://data.doi.gov/dataset/blm-natl-aim-terradat-hub-ae503
  • What it adds. Probabilistic, standardized rangeland indicators (bare ground, vegetation composition / height, canopy gap, invasive plants) on BLM lands — useful as environmental covariates for vector habitat models. The BLM Arizona Strip District implements treatments to manage habitat and grazing.

U.S. Forest Service (USFS)

  • Coronado, Coconino, Kaibab, Prescott, Tonto, Apache-Sitgreaves national forests cover a substantial portion of AZ wildlands; mortality and zoonotic events on USFS land typically route to AZGFD and county health departments.

Academic and research institutions

National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON)

  • Website: https://www.neonscience.org/
  • Data portal: https://data.neonscience.org/
  • Operator: Battelle. Funded by the NSF. 81 field sites across 20 ecoclimatic domains with a 30-year observatory mission.
  • Arizona sites in NEON Domain 14 (Desert Southwest).
    • Santa Rita Experimental Range (SRER) core terrestrial site (UA SRER NEON page). Six 40 × 40 m Distributed Tick Plots co-located with Distributed Base Plots; ten Distributed Mosquito Points outside the Tower Airshed.
  • Relevant standardized data products.
    • Mosquito CO₂ light-trap sampling — adult abundance and species.
    • Tick drag-cloth sampling — abundance by life stage.
    • Tick-borne pathogen status — Borrelia, Anaplasma, Babesia, Rickettsia on collected ticks.
    • Small mammal box trapping — rodent abundance, species, body condition, recapture.
    • Rodent-borne pathogen screening.
    • Bird grids and point counts — community-level abundance.
  • NEON Biorepository (managed by Arizona State University). Physical samples archived for community research, including voucher specimens of carabids and mosquitoes plus DNA extracts from beetles, ticks, and mosquitoes. Battelle / ASU Biorepository overview.
  • Access. Free data portal, NEON Data API, neonUtilities (R), neonutilities (Python).

University of Arizona — Mel & Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health (MEZCOPH)

  • One Health Initiative: https://publichealth.arizona.edu/one-health-initiative
  • One Health Research: https://publichealth.arizona.edu/about/strategic-initiatives/one-health/research
  • 2026 Southwest One Health Symposium: https://publichealth.arizona.edu/about/strategic-initiatives/one-health/events/2026-symposium
  • Role. Lead UA One Health initiative bridging ~10 colleges and 20+ departments (Public Health, Medicine, Veterinary Sciences). Frank von Hippel leads the One Health Research Initiative.

University of Arizona Cooperative Extension — Great Arizona Tick Check

  • Program page: https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check
  • Help page: https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check/help-us
  • Spring 2024 update: https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check/spring-2024-update
  • Announcement: https://news.arizona.edu/news/uarizona-researchers-launch-great-arizona-tick-check
  • Why this matters. This is the closest thing Arizona has to a statewide participatory tick-surveillance program — and it precisely models what the EpiHack focus group is trying to build.
  • Mechanics. Members of the public mail in ticks they find on themselves, animals, or in the environment. Dr. Kathleen Walker’s lab in the Department of Entomology identifies the tick, screens for pathogens, and provides feedback to the sender on health risks and control options.
  • Submission address. Dr. Kathleen Walker, Forbes 410, Department of Entomology, P.O. Box 210036, University of Arizona, Tucson, AZ 85721.
  • What to submit. Tick in a sealed bag (recommended frozen 1–2 days) plus a sheet noting date collected, city / town, and ZIP code.
  • Goal. First-ever Arizona database of tick distribution and associated diseases.
  • Findings to date. ~200 samples and at least six tick species identified; Gulf Coast ticks discovered in Cochise and Santa Cruz counties; Western black-legged ticks (Lyme vector) collected and received from Mohave County. The brown dog tick remains the dominant statewide species and the principal RMSF vector.
  • Funding & partners. ~$1M CDC grant. Collaboration of UA Cooperative Extension, MEZCOPH, and ADHS.
  • Direct fit with the participatory framework. Treat this as the proven channel for tick data; the new system should ingest from / not duplicate / feed back into the Great Arizona Tick Check.

Arizona Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (AZVDL)

  • Website: https://azvdl.arizona.edu/
  • Zoonotic-disease activity: https://azvdl.arizona.edu/news/combatting-spread-zoonotic-diseases
  • Role. UA College of Veterinary Medicine. AAVLD-accredited and a member of the USDA NAHLN (Level 2). Conducts ~13,000 tests per year including necropsy, biopsy, bacterial culture, serology, parasitology, and molecular diagnostics.
  • Surveillance focus. Foreign / emerging animal diseases (FMD, classical swine fever, HPAI, Newcastle Disease) and routine veterinary diagnostics.
  • One Health. Member of the Arizona One Health collaborative.

Northern Arizona University — Pathogen and Microbiome Institute (PMI)

  • Website: https://in.nau.edu/pmi/
  • Plague work. Dr. Dave Wagner leads world-class Yersinia pestis (plague) genomic research. NAU PMI partners with TGen North in Flagstaff for pathogen sequencing. Recent commentary on a 2025 human plague case in Coconino County.
  • TGen (Translational Genomics Research Institute): https://www.tgen.org/ — Phoenix-based; partner on pathogen genomics.

Arizona State University — Biodesign Institute

  • Website: https://biodesign.asu.edu/
  • Notable program. Wastewater-based epidemiology research (Rolf Halden et al.) — primarily human-pathogen focused but the methodology generalizes to zoonotic spillover detection at population scale.
  • NEON Biorepository is also hosted at ASU.

Participatory and citizen-science programs

Great Arizona Tick Check

Detailed above under Academic — this is the flagship AZ-specific participatory program for vector data.

iNaturalist

  • Website: https://www.inaturalist.org/
  • Scale. 200+ million observations from 3.3+ million observers worldwide (as of late 2024). Strong AZ user community.
  • Why it matters. Existing infrastructure for photo + geolocation + species ID that the participatory-surveillance system can either federate with or use as a model. Observations are published to GBIF and free for research re-use.

eBird

  • Website: https://ebird.org/
  • Operator. Cornell Lab of Ornithology.
  • Why it matters. The dominant bird-observation citizen-science platform; complementary to iNaturalist for HPAI early-warning in wild birds. Cross-platform analyses confirm seasonality patterns match for >97% of species studied.

Outbreaks Near Me

  • Website: https://outbreaksnearme.org/us/en-US
  • Operator. Boston Children’s Hospital + HealthMap (Dr. John Brownstein’s team). Successor to Flu Near You (2011–2022).
  • What it is. A US-wide participatory-surveillance platform where the public self-reports symptoms — fever, cough, GI signs, rashes, fatigue — and the platform aggregates the data by ZIP code into a near-real-time symptom-cluster map. Free, no account beyond a ZIP and an age band.
  • Why it matters. It is the closest existing analogue to the AZ One Health Sentinel app’s symptom-side intake; the AZ prototype should either federate with it (handing OBNM-style symptom reports straight through), or — at minimum — direct users to it for human symptom self-reporting while the Sentinel app focuses on the wildlife / vector / heat side that OBNM doesn’t cover. The HealthMap team’s prior work (BioCaster, ProMED-mail, HealthMap.org) is also worth benchmarking against for the Cluster Detection Agent.
  • Where it lands in the knowledge graph: resource.outbreaks_near_me node in schema/deep/datasets_apis.sql with edges to focus.zoonotic, focus.vector_borne, and the Figure-3 Detect milestone (it directly improves early detection).

AZGFD wildlife mortality reporting

See the AZGFD entry above. AZGFD’s existing public-report channel for sick / dead wildlife is the de facto Arizona participatory baseline; a new system should integrate with it rather than compete.

Maricopa County “Fight the Bite”

  • Website: https://fightthebitemaricopa.org/
  • Public-facing map and prevention messaging hub that complements the MCESD trap network. A model for community communication.
