Wildlife & Vector-Borne Diseases

EpiHack Arizona 2026 focus group — building participatory surveillance for wildlife disease and vector-borne pathogens across Arizona's One Health landscape.

The four guiding questions

1How does Arizona track wildlife and vector density? AZGFD wildlife surveillance, county mosquito programs, tick and flea gaps, rodent monitoring. 2How does Arizona monitor wildlife / vectors for zoonotic infections? Arbovirus pools, plague flea testing, CWD / HPAI, NEON pathogen testing. 3What technologies could improve surveillance in this sector? eDNA, metagenomics, smart traps, acoustic monitoring, knowledge graphs, LLM intake. 4How can wildlife diseases be better tracked using Participatory Surveillance? Data resources and collection programs full catalog →

Programs that hold data relevant to wildlife and vector-borne disease surveillance in Arizona, organized by jurisdiction. The full resources page has the complete catalog; the cards below highlight the most relevant anchors per category.

State of Arizona

Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS) state · public-health authority

Vector-Borne and Zoonotic Diseases program — WNV, SLE, dengue, Zika, hantavirus (6 cases in 2023, 11 in 2024), plague, tularemia, rabies, RMSF. Operates the Arizona State Public Health Laboratory and arboviral syndromic surveillance. Co-author of the Arizona One Health Toolkit.

azdhs.gov

Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD) state · wildlife authority

Wildlife Health Program. CWD surveillance > 25,000 samples since 1998 (1,200–1,600/year); HPAI wild-bird monitoring; plague die-off response; existing public channel for reporting sick or dead wildlife.

azgfd.com / Wildlife Diseases

Counties

Maricopa County (MCDPH + MCESD Vector Control) county

800+ vector traps county-wide; mosquito pools tested for WNV and SLE; weekly Vector Index updates every Friday. Public site Fight the Bite Maricopa.

maricopa.gov / Vector Control · fightthebitemaricopa.org

Pima County Health Department — Vector Control county

Mosquito surveillance May–November focused on Culex tarsalis and Culex quinquefasciatus; free Gila topminnow biological control program.

pima.gov / Vector Control

Tribal nations

Arizona is home to 22 federally recognized tribal nations. Many run their own health, natural-resource, and veterinary programs that hold data not visible in state-level reports. A participatory system must engage tribal partners as co-designers.

Navajo Epidemiology Center tribal

Established 2005. Manages Navajo Nation public-health information systems; runs the Navajo Nation Health Survey. Critical context for hantavirus — Sin Nombre virus was first characterized during the 1993 Four Corners outbreak.

nec.navajo-nsn.gov

Inter Tribal Council of Arizona — Tribal Epidemiology Center tribal

Builds tribally-driven public-health and epidemiologic capacity across the Phoenix and Tucson IHS Areas.

itcaonline.com / Epidemiology

Federal

USGS National Wildlife Health Center — WHISPers federal

National wildlife mortality / morbidity event repository and the portal for NWHC diagnostic services. Routinely partners with state, tribal, and federal agencies on disease surveillance.

usgs.gov/centers/nwhc · whispers.usgs.gov

USDA APHIS Wildlife Services (Arizona) federal

Predation management, urban coyotes, invasive species, and zoonotic diseases — especially plague and rabies. Operates the national Surveillance and Emergency Response System (SERS).

aphis.usda.gov/wildlife-services

U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service (Arizona offices) federal

Administers ~1.8M acres in AZ via refuges and hatcheries. HPAI monitoring on Service lands; Arizona received $134,320 via the ARPA Zoonotic Disease Grant Program for interagency coordination.

fws.gov / Arizona Ecological Services

National Park Service · BLM · USFS (Arizona) federal · land managers

Grand Canyon and Saguaro NPS units coordinate with county HHS on hantavirus, plague, WNV, and rabies. BLM AIM / TerrADat provides standardized rangeland vegetation indicators that double as vector-habitat covariates.

Academic and research

National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON) federal-funded research

NSF-funded, Battelle-operated 30-year observatory with standardized mosquito, tick, rodent, and bird sampling across 20 ecoclimatic domains. Arizona is in Domain 14 (Desert Southwest); the Santa Rita Experimental Range hosts six tick plots and ten mosquito points. NEON Biorepository is hosted at ASU.

neonscience.org · data.neonscience.org

UA Cooperative Extension — Great Arizona Tick Check academic · participatory

Arizona's flagship participatory tick-surveillance program. Anyone in AZ can mail in ticks they find on themselves, animals, or in the environment. Dr. Kathleen Walker's lab at UA Entomology identifies the tick, screens for pathogens, and sends the submitter feedback on risk and control. Funded by a ~$1M CDC grant; partners with ADHS and UA MEZCOPH.

Already discovered Gulf Coast ticks in Cochise and Santa Cruz counties and Western black-legged ticks (Lyme vector) in Mohave County.

Mail to: Dr. Kathleen Walker, Forbes 410, Dept. of Entomology, P.O. Box 210036, University of Arizona, Tucson, AZ 85721.

extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check

UA Mel & Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health — One Health Initiative academic

Bridges ~10 UA colleges and 20+ departments on One Health research and education. Hosts the Southwest One Health Symposium.

publichealth.arizona.edu / One Health

AZVDL · NAU PMI · TGen · ASU Biodesign academic · diagnostics & genomics

Arizona Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (AAVLD-accredited, NAHLN Level 2, ~13,000 tests/year, HPAI surveillance). NAU Pathogen and Microbiome Institute (Dave Wagner — world-class plague genomics). TGen North (pathogen sequencing partner). ASU Biodesign (wastewater epidemiology; hosts the NEON Biorepository).

Citizen-science platforms

iNaturalist · eBird · GBIF citizen science

iNaturalist (200M+ observations, 3.3M+ observers globally) and eBird (Cornell) are the dominant biodiversity observation platforms and existing infrastructure for photo + geolocation + species ID. Both publish to GBIF.

inaturalist.org · ebird.org · gbif.org
