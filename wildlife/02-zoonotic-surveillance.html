Q2 — How does Arizona monitor wildlife / vectors for zoonotic infections?

How does Arizona monitor wildlife and/or vectors for presence or absence of zoonotic infections?

Active surveillance (testing without a human/animal case)

Pathogen Primary host / vector tested Agency
West Nile virus (WNV) Mosquito pools (mainly Culex) County vector control → ADHS
St. Louis encephalitis virus Mosquito pools County vector control → ADHS
Aedes aegypti presence Adult trapping, ovitrap surveillance County vector control
Plague (Yersinia pestis) Flea pools from prairie-dog / rodent die-offs ADHS Vector-Borne & Zoonotic Diseases
Hantavirus (Sin Nombre) Trapped rodents (mostly research / NEON) NEON, university labs
Avian influenza (HPAI) Wild waterfowl, raptors, mortality events AZGFD, USDA Wildlife Services
Chronic wasting disease (CWD) Hunter-harvested deer / elk (lymph nodes) AZGFD

Passive / case-driven surveillance

  • Human cases of WNV, hantavirus pulmonary syndrome, plague, RMSF, tularemia, and rabies are nationally notifiable and reported to ADHS.
  • Animal rabies cases (skunks, bats, foxes) reported by submitters to the Arizona State Public Health Laboratory.
  • Veterinary diagnostic submissions (livestock, wildlife rehab) feed into ad-hoc situational awareness.

NEON — the standardized layer

NEON adds a research-grade, longitudinal layer to Arizona zoonotic surveillance that complements the agency case-driven work:

  • Ticks collected by drag cloth are pooled and tested for tick-borne pathogens.
  • Small mammals captured in box traps have a subset of blood and ear punches archived; a subset is screened for rodent-borne pathogens.
  • Mosquitoes are sampled with CO₂ light traps and identified to species; pathogen testing is not part of the standard NEON product but archived material is available to researchers.
  • All data is openly published via the NEON data portal.

Gaps for the participatory-surveillance design

  • Few cross-agency dashboards: WNV, plague, CWD each live in their own silo.
  • No standing wildlife-rabies seroprevalence surveillance outside reactive case work.
  • Tick-pathogen status is invisible to the public until a human case triggers a county investigation.
