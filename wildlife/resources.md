# Wildlife & Vector-Borne Disease — Data Resources & Collection Programs

A working catalog of agencies and programs that collect or hold data
relevant to wildlife and vector-borne disease surveillance in Arizona,
organized by jurisdiction. Each entry includes top-level URLs, the
program's data products, and how it can plug into a participatory
surveillance system and the DuckLake knowledge graph.

## State of Arizona

### Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS)
- **Website:** <https://www.azdhs.gov/>
- **Vector-Borne & Zoonotic Diseases program:**
  <https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/vector-borne-zoonotic-diseases/>
- **Mosquito-borne program:**
  <https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/mosquito-borne/>
- **Role.** Statewide public-health authority. Operates the Vector-Borne
  & Zoonotic Disease (VBZD) team that works with the Arizona Department
  of Agriculture and AZGFD on cross-sector animal disease events. Owns
  the Arizona State Public Health Laboratory.
- **Surveillance.** West Nile virus, St. Louis encephalitis, dengue,
  Zika, **hantavirus** (spike of 6 cases in 2023 and 11 in 2024 with six
  deaths), **plague**, **tularemia**, **rabies**, Rocky Mountain spotted
  fever (active 200+ owned/stray dog seroprevalence project in Cochise,
  Santa Cruz, and Yuma counties). Uses **syndromic surveillance** for
  rapid identification of arboviral cases.
- **Documents.** The
  [Arizona One Health Toolkit (2019)](https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/vector-borne-zoonotic-diseases/arizona-one-health-toolkit-2019.pdf)
  is the formal multi-agency coordination playbook.

### Arizona Game and Fish Department (AZGFD)
- **Website:** <https://www.azgfd.com/>
- **Wildlife Diseases page:** <https://www.azgfd.com/wildlife-conservation/wildlife-diseases-2/>
- **Wildlife Conservation Strategy — Disease chapter:**
  <https://awcs.azgfd.com/conservation-challenges/disease-pathogens-and-parasites>
- **Role.** Wildlife data authority for Arizona. Investigates significant
  wildlife morbidity and mortality events.
- **Chronic Wasting Disease (CWD).** Not yet detected in Arizona despite
  bordering three CWD-positive states. AZGFD has tested **25,000+
  samples since 1998**, collecting ~1,200–1,600 samples per year via
  check stations, hunt camps, taxidermists, game-meat processors, and
  hunter submissions.
- **HPAI / avian influenza.** Active wild-bird surveillance.
- **Other programs.** Plague die-off response in prairie-dog colonies,
  bighorn pneumonia, brucellosis, mange.
- **Public reporting.** Public can report dead or sick wildlife to the
  Wildlife Health Program directly.

### Arizona Department of Agriculture
- **Website:** <https://agriculture.az.gov/>
- **Role.** Animal-industry regulator. Coordinates with AZGFD and ADHS
  on cross-sector zoonoses (e.g. brucellosis in livestock, HPAI in
  domestic poultry interfaces).

### Arizona One Health (multi-agency initiative)
- **Coordinating reference:**
  [Arizona One Health Toolkit (2019)](https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/vector-borne-zoonotic-diseases/arizona-one-health-toolkit-2019.pdf)
- **Partners include:** ADHS, AZGFD, Arizona Department of Agriculture,
  USDA APHIS Wildlife Services, USDA APHIS Veterinary Services, AZVDL,
  CDC, University of Arizona.

## Arizona counties

### Maricopa County
- **Vector Control (MCESD):**
  <https://www.maricopa.gov/632/Vector-Control>
- **Public dashboard / data:**
  <https://www.maricopa.gov/2476/Mosquito-Borne-Disease-Statistics>
- **Public-facing site:** <https://fightthebitemaricopa.org/>
- **Vector-borne & zoonotic reports:**
  <https://www.maricopa.gov/4236/Vector-borne-Zoonotic-Reports>
- **Network.** Over **800 vector traps** county-wide, mosquito pools
  tested for **WNV and SLE**, with a published weekly **Vector Index**
  (VI) that estimates the proportion of infected mosquitoes per species
  per area. Data is updated **weekly on Fridays**.
- **Notable.** Maricopa County had an "unprecedented" 2021 West Nile
  outbreak documented in
  [CDC MMWR](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7217a1.htm).

### Pima County Health Department
- **Vector Control Program:** <https://www.pima.gov/2296/Vector-Control-Program>
- **Mosquitoes:** <https://www.pima.gov/2100/Mosquitoes>
- **Trapping window.** May through November; focus on
  *Culex tarsalis* and *Culex quinquefasciatus*.
- **Additional program.** Free **Gila topminnow** distribution program
  (a native fish) for biological mosquito control in approved water
  bodies such as golf-course ponds.
- **Hotline.** 520-724-7908.

### Other county programs
County health departments in **Coconino, Yavapai, Yuma, Pinal, Mohave,
Cochise, Santa Cruz, Apache, Navajo, La Paz, Greenlee, Graham, Gila**
each run their own surveillance to varying scales; most feed weekly
summaries into ADHS during arbovirus season.

## Tribal nations and tribal epidemiology centers

Arizona is home to **22 federally recognized tribal nations**. Many run
their own health, natural-resource, and veterinary programs that hold
data not otherwise visible in state-level reports. Any wildlife /
vector-borne participatory system must engage tribal partners as
co-designers, not as data sources.

### The 22 federally recognized tribes in Arizona
Ak-Chin Indian Community · Cocopah Indian Tribe · Colorado River Indian
Tribes · Fort McDowell Yavapai Nation · Fort Mojave Indian Tribe ·
Fort Yuma Quechan Tribe · Gila River Indian Community · Havasupai Tribe
· Hopi Tribe · Hualapai Tribe · Kaibab Band of Paiute Indians · Navajo
Nation · Pascua Yaqui Tribe · Pueblo of Zuni · Salt River
Pima-Maricopa Indian Community · San Carlos Apache Tribe · San Juan
Southern Paiute Tribe · Tohono O'odham Nation · Tonto Apache Tribe ·
White Mountain Apache Tribe · Yavapai-Apache Nation · Yavapai-Prescott
Indian Tribe.

### Tribal Epidemiology Centers (TECs)

#### Navajo Epidemiology Center (NEC)
- **Website:** <https://nec.navajo-nsn.gov/>
- **Profile:** <https://tribalepicenters.org/navajo-epidemiology-center/>
- **Established:** 2005. Manages the Navajo Nation's public-health
  information systems, runs the Navajo Nation Health Survey (NNHS — a
  Navajo-specific BRFSS adaptation), and investigates diseases and
  injuries of concern. Critical context for **hantavirus** surveillance
  — Sin Nombre virus was first characterized during the 1993 Four
  Corners outbreak.
- **2025 agreement.** Signed a Memorandum of Agreement with the New
  Mexico Department of Health for stronger cross-jurisdiction data
  sharing.

#### Inter Tribal Council of Arizona — Tribal Epidemiology Center (ITCA-TEC)
- **Website:** <https://itcaonline.com/programs/research-and-evaluation/epidemiology/>
- **Profile:** <https://tribalepicenters.org/inter-tribal-council-of-arizona-tribal-epidemiology-center/>
- **Role.** Builds tribally-driven public-health and epidemiologic
  capacity across the **Phoenix and Tucson IHS Areas**. Based in
  Phoenix.

### Tribal natural-resource and health departments (selected examples)

- **Navajo Nation Department of Fish and Wildlife** — wildlife
  authority for the largest reservation in the U.S. (parts of AZ, NM,
  UT). Plague is endemic; rodent and flea die-offs are tracked locally.
- **Tohono O'odham Nation Health & Human Services:**
  <https://www.tonation-nsn.gov/health-human-services/> — serves ~28,000
  members across 2.8 million acres in southwestern Arizona.
  **Sells Indian Hospital** (IHS) is the principal facility.
- **Hopi Tribe**, **Hualapai Tribe**, **Havasupai Tribe** — northern AZ
  wildlife / vector contexts overlap with Coconino County's hantavirus,
  plague, and RMSF surveillance.
- **White Mountain Apache Tribe** and **San Carlos Apache Tribe** —
  large reservations with significant wildlife populations.
- **Pascua Yaqui Tribe** (Tucson area) and **Gila River Indian
  Community** / **Salt River Pima-Maricopa Indian Community** (Phoenix
  area) — urban-edge wildlife and vector exposure.

### Indian Health Service (IHS)
- **Phoenix Area:** <https://www.ihs.gov/phoenix/> — covers AZ, NV, UT
  and serves ~180,000 American Indian / Alaska Native users.
- **Tucson Area:** services include Sells Indian Hospital.

- **American Rescue Plan Act Zoonotic Disease Grant Program:**
  <https://www.fws.gov/project/american-rescue-plan-act-zoonotic-disease-grant-program>

### National Park Service (NPS)
- **Grand Canyon — Zoonotic Diseases in Wildlife:**
  <https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/nature/zoonotic_diseases.htm>
- Coconino County HHS partners with NPS at Grand Canyon for
  **hantavirus, plague, West Nile virus, and rabies** surveillance.
  Recent confirmed-rabies bat collected near Kanab Creek Canyon.
- **Saguaro National Park** — investigated wildlife mortality and
  unusual behavior (foxes; potential rabies link) in collaboration with
  Pima County and ADHS.

### Bureau of Land Management (BLM)
- **Assessment, Inventory, and Monitoring (AIM):**
  <https://www.blm.gov/aim>
- **TerrADat (national terrestrial AIM database hub):**
  <https://data.doi.gov/dataset/blm-natl-aim-terradat-hub-ae503>
- **What it adds.** Probabilistic, standardized rangeland indicators
  (bare ground, vegetation composition / height, canopy gap, invasive
  plants) on BLM lands — useful as environmental covariates for vector
  habitat models. The
  [BLM Arizona Strip District](https://www.blm.gov/) implements treatments
  to manage habitat and grazing.

### U.S. Forest Service (USFS)
- Coronado, Coconino, Kaibab, Prescott, Tonto, Apache-Sitgreaves
  national forests cover a substantial portion of AZ wildlands; mortality
  and zoonotic events on USFS land typically route to AZGFD and county
  health departments.

## Academic and research institutions

### National Ecological Observatory Network (NEON)
- **Website:** <https://www.neonscience.org/>
- **Data portal:** <https://data.neonscience.org/>
- **Operator:** Battelle. Funded by the **NSF**. **81 field sites
  across 20 ecoclimatic domains** with a 30-year observatory mission.
- **Arizona sites in NEON Domain 14 (Desert Southwest).**
  - **Santa Rita Experimental Range (SRER)** core terrestrial site
    ([UA SRER NEON page](https://santarita.arizona.edu/data-resources/data-download/national-ecological-observatory-network-neon/neon-observational)).
    Six 40 × 40 m **Distributed Tick Plots** co-located with Distributed
    Base Plots; ten **Distributed Mosquito Points** outside the Tower
    Airshed.
- **Relevant standardized data products.**
  - Mosquito CO₂ light-trap sampling — adult abundance and species.
  - Tick drag-cloth sampling — abundance by life stage.
  - Tick-borne pathogen status — *Borrelia*, *Anaplasma*, *Babesia*,
    *Rickettsia* on collected ticks.
  - Small mammal box trapping — rodent abundance, species, body
    condition, recapture.
  - Rodent-borne pathogen screening.
  - Bird grids and point counts — community-level abundance.
- **NEON Biorepository (managed by Arizona State University).**
  Physical samples archived for community research, including voucher
  specimens of carabids and mosquitoes plus DNA extracts from beetles,
  ticks, and mosquitoes.
  [Battelle / ASU Biorepository overview](https://entomologytoday.org/2020/06/18/national-ecological-observatory-network-biorepository-neon-insects-arthropods-vector-borne-disease-entomology/).
- **Access.** Free data portal, NEON Data API, `neonUtilities`
  (R), `neonutilities` (Python).

### University of Arizona — Mel & Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health (MEZCOPH)
- **One Health Initiative:**
  <https://publichealth.arizona.edu/one-health-initiative>
- **One Health Research:**
  <https://publichealth.arizona.edu/about/strategic-initiatives/one-health/research>
- **2026 Southwest One Health Symposium:**
  <https://publichealth.arizona.edu/about/strategic-initiatives/one-health/events/2026-symposium>
- **Role.** Lead UA One Health initiative bridging ~10 colleges and

  Frank von Hippel leads the One Health Research Initiative.

### University of Arizona Cooperative Extension — Great Arizona Tick Check
- **Program page:** <https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check>
- **Help page:** <https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check/help-us>
- **Spring 2024 update:** <https://extension.arizona.edu/programs/great-arizona-tick-check/spring-2024-update>
- **Announcement:** <https://news.arizona.edu/news/uarizona-researchers-launch-great-arizona-tick-check>
- **Why this matters.** This is the closest thing Arizona has to a
  **statewide participatory tick-surveillance program** — and it
  precisely models what the EpiHack focus group is trying to build.
- **Mechanics.** Members of the public mail in ticks they find on
  themselves, animals, or in the environment. Dr. **Kathleen Walker**'s
  lab in the Department of Entomology identifies the tick, screens for
  pathogens, and provides feedback to the sender on health risks and
  control options.
- **Submission address.**
  Dr. Kathleen Walker, Forbes 410, Department of Entomology,
  P.O. Box 210036, University of Arizona, Tucson, AZ 85721.
- **What to submit.** Tick in a sealed bag (recommended frozen 1–2
  days) plus a sheet noting **date collected**, **city / town**, and
  **ZIP code**.
- **Goal.** First-ever Arizona database of tick distribution and
  associated diseases.
- **Findings to date.** ~200 samples and at least six tick species
  identified; **Gulf Coast ticks** discovered in Cochise and Santa
  Cruz counties; **Western black-legged ticks** (Lyme vector)
  collected and received from Mohave County. The brown dog tick
  remains the dominant statewide species and the principal RMSF vector.
- **Funding & partners.** ~$1M CDC grant. Collaboration of **UA
  Cooperative Extension, MEZCOPH, and ADHS**.
- **Direct fit with the participatory framework.** Treat this as the
  proven channel for tick data; the new system should ingest from /
  not duplicate / feed back into the Great Arizona Tick Check.

### Arizona Veterinary Diagnostic Laboratory (AZVDL)
- **Website:** <https://azvdl.arizona.edu/>
- **Zoonotic-disease activity:**
  <https://azvdl.arizona.edu/news/combatting-spread-zoonotic-diseases>
- **Role.** UA College of Veterinary Medicine. **AAVLD-accredited** and
  a member of the USDA **NAHLN** (Level 2). Conducts ~13,000 tests per
  year including necropsy, biopsy, bacterial culture, serology,
  parasitology, and molecular diagnostics.
- **Surveillance focus.** Foreign / emerging animal diseases (FMD,
  classical swine fever, **HPAI**, Newcastle Disease) and routine
  veterinary diagnostics.
- **One Health.** Member of the Arizona One Health collaborative.

### Northern Arizona University — Pathogen and Microbiome Institute (PMI)
- **Website:** <https://in.nau.edu/pmi/>
- **Plague work.** Dr. **Dave Wagner** leads world-class *Yersinia
  pestis* (plague) genomic research. NAU PMI partners with
  **TGen North** in Flagstaff for pathogen sequencing. Recent commentary
  on
  [a 2025 human plague case in Coconino County](https://www.knau.org/knau-and-arizona-news/2025-07-18/nau-scientist-discusses-rare-human-case-of-plague-in-coconino-county).
- **TGen (Translational Genomics Research Institute):**
  <https://www.tgen.org/> — Phoenix-based; partner on pathogen genomics.

### Arizona State University — Biodesign Institute
- **Website:** <https://biodesign.asu.edu/>
- **Notable program.** Wastewater-based epidemiology research (Rolf
  Halden et al.) — primarily human-pathogen focused but the
  methodology generalizes to zoonotic spillover detection at
  population scale.
- **NEON Biorepository** is also hosted at ASU.

## Participatory and citizen-science programs

### Great Arizona Tick Check
*Detailed above under Academic — this is the flagship AZ-specific
participatory program for vector data.*

### iNaturalist
- **Website:** <https://www.inaturalist.org/>
- **Scale.** 200+ million observations from 3.3+ million observers
  worldwide (as of late 2024). Strong AZ user community.
- **Why it matters.** Existing infrastructure for **photo +
  geolocation + species ID** that the participatory-surveillance
  system can either federate with or use as a model. Observations are
  published to **GBIF** and free for research re-use.

### eBird
- **Website:** <https://ebird.org/>
- **Operator.** Cornell Lab of Ornithology.
- **Why it matters.** The dominant bird-observation citizen-science
  platform; complementary to iNaturalist for HPAI early-warning in
  wild birds. Cross-platform analyses confirm seasonality patterns
  match for >97% of species studied.

### AZGFD wildlife mortality reporting
*See the AZGFD entry above. AZGFD's existing public-report channel for
sick / dead wildlife is the de facto Arizona participatory baseline; a
new system should integrate with it rather than compete.*

### Maricopa County "Fight the Bite"
- **Website:** <https://fightthebitemaricopa.org/>
- **Public-facing** map and prevention messaging hub that complements
  the MCESD trap network. A model for community communication.
