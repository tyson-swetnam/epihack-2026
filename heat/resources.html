State of Arizona

Arizona Department of Health Services (ADHS) — Heat Safety / Climate & Health

  • Heat Safety landing: https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/extreme-weather/heat-safety/
  • Heat Preparedness: https://www.azdhs.gov/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/extreme-weather/heat-safety/heat-preparedness/index.php
  • ADHS Heat Preparedness Network map (ArcGIS Experience): https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/c5bdf9ab90894e1baa5860c450dedb3b — statewide map of Heat Preparedness Network locations.
  • Injury Mortality (heat) report portal: https://pub.azdhs.gov/health-stats/report/heat/
  • Heat-Caused & Heat-Related Deaths (2012–2023) report (PDF): https://www.azdhs.gov/documents/preparedness/epidemiology-disease-control/extreme-weather/pubs/heat-related-mortality-year-2012-2023.pdf
  • Climate & Health Program — established 2013 under the CDC BRACE cooperative agreement; produces climate-and-health profile, vulnerability assessments, intervention assessments, and adaptation plans.
  • Surveillance. Heat-related illness emergency-department and inpatient admissions; data updated regularly during heat season (May–October); facility reporting through the CDC National Syndromic Surveillance Program (BioSense).
  • Burden. >4,320 heat-exposure deaths in AZ 2013–2024; ~4,298 ED visits / year; 990 deaths in 2023.

Arizona Governor’s Extreme Heat Preparedness Plan

  • Annual statewide plan launched at the start of each heat season (May 1). Coordinates ADHS, ADES, ADOT, MAG, county HHS, and city heat offices. 2025/2026 versions announced by Gov. Hobbs.
  • Emergency Information Network: https://ein.az.gov/2026-heat-relief-network-launches-may-1-protect-residents-during-extreme-heat

Arizona Department of Economic Security (ADES)

  • LIHEAP (Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program) — federal utility assistance administered by the state through community action agencies. Critical in heat season for AC bill relief and emergency AC repair.

Counties and cities

Maricopa County Department of Public Health (MCDPH) — Heat Surveillance

  • Heat Reports / Surveillance: https://www.maricopa.gov/1858/Heat-Surveillance
  • Program. Heat-associated mortality surveillance system established 2005/2006; tracks cause-of-death circumstances for residents and visitors. Annual Heat-Associated Deaths in Maricopa County report.
  • Real-time updates during heat season (May–October) via online dashboard; CDC MMWR article documents the 2021 unprecedented WNV outbreak and a 2023 cooling-center access study (MMWR 2025).

Maricopa Association of Governments (MAG) — Heat Relief Network

  • Interactive map: https://hrn.azmag.gov/
  • Program landing: https://azmag.gov/Programs/Heat-Relief-Network
  • Resources / supply drive: https://azmag.gov/Programs/Heat-Relief-Network/Resources
  • Scope. 200+ cooling, hydration, respite, and donation drop-off locations across Maricopa County, May 1 – September 30, since 2005. Filters by services, hours, and pet-friendly. Partner network includes governments, nonprofits, faith-based groups, and businesses.

City of Phoenix — Office of Heat Response and Mitigation (OHRM / HeatReadyPHX)

  • Office landing: https://www.phoenix.gov/heat
  • Department page: https://www.phoenix.gov/administration/departments/heat.html
  • First publicly-funded municipal heat office in the U.S. (2021).
  • Director: Dr. David Hondula (ASU). Runs HeatReadyPHX, tree-and-shade master plans, cool-pavement pilots, reflective bus-shelter roof studies.

Pima County

  • Cooling Centers map: https://www.pima.gov/2307/Cooling-Centers
  • Stay Informed During Extreme Heat: https://www.pima.gov/3225/Stay-Informed-Extreme-Heat
  • Scope. ~3 dozen air-conditioned cooling centers and hydration stations stocked with cooling towels, water bottles, electrolyte packets, and education materials.

City of Tempe — Heat Relief

  • https://www.tempe.gov/government/community-health-and-human-services/housing-services/ending-homelessness/heat-relief
  • Tempe specifically links heat relief to homelessness services.

Other cities and counties

Tucson (City), Mesa, Chandler, Scottsdale, Glendale, Peoria, Flagstaff, Yuma, and other municipalities operate their own cooling centers and hydration stations during the season, generally listed on the MAG HRN map for the Phoenix metro and on county / city pages elsewhere.

Tribal nations

Inter Tribal Council of Arizona — Tribal Epidemiology Center (ITCA-TEC)

  • Profile: https://itcaonline.com/programs/research-and-evaluation/epidemiology/
  • Builds tribally-driven epidemiologic capacity across the Phoenix and Tucson IHS Areas; partner with CLIMAS on tribal heat resilience.

CLIMAS (Climate Assessment for the Southwest) — UA + ITCA + NMSU

  • NOAA-funded partnership (since 1998) explicitly working on rural, border, and tribal heat resilience.
  • Partner: https://climas.arizona.edu/

Navajo Epidemiology Center (NEC)

  • https://nec.navajo-nsn.gov/
  • Heat exposure compounds with housing-quality gaps on the Navajo Nation; ITCA-TEC and NEC are natural design partners for any tribal-inclusive heat surveillance.

22 federally recognized AZ tribes

Listed in the wildlife resources page. Tribal natural-resource and HHS departments hold local heat-impact context not visible in state-level reports.

Federal agencies

CDC

  • Climate-Ready States & Cities Initiative (BRACE). Arizona is one of 16 states / 2 cities in the program; ADHS Climate & Health Program is the AZ recipient. UA MEZCOPH received $2M in CDC funding for AZ heat interventions.
  • National Syndromic Surveillance Program (NSSP) / BioSense. AZ ED heat-illness data flows here.
  • CDC ATSDR — Place and Health — Extreme Heat Adaptation: https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/place-health/share/extreme-heat-adaptation.html
  • MMWR 2025: Assessment of Awareness, Use, and Access Barriers to Cooling Centers in Maricopa County, Arizona — August 1–September 15, 2023: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/wr/mm7414a4.htm.

NOAA — National Weather Service

  • NWS Phoenix: https://www.weather.gov/psr/
  • NWS Phoenix Heat page: https://www.weather.gov/psr/heat
  • NWS Tucson: https://www.weather.gov/twc/
  • HeatRisk — daily ZIP-code-level color-coded forecast (Green / Yellow / Orange / Red / Magenta). Triggers Extreme Heat Watch and Extreme Heat Warning products.

NIHHIS — National Integrated Heat Health Information System (NOAA + HHS)

  • Federal cross-agency coordination on heat health.
  • Funds the UA-led Center for Heat Resilient Communities.

Department of Energy — Southwest Urban Corridor Integrated Field Laboratory (SW-IFL)

  • DOE-funded heat / climate field laboratory anchored in the Phoenix–Tucson urban corridor. UA partner.

Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD)

  • Continuum of Care (CoC) data for sheltered / unsheltered populations — relevant for the unsheltered cohort that bears the heaviest heat-mortality burden.

Academic and research institutions

University of Arizona — Heat Resilience Initiative

  • Website: https://heat.arizona.edu/
  • Research programs: https://heat.arizona.edu/research/research-programs
  • Umbrella that coordinates:
    • DOE-funded Southwest Urban Corridor Integrated Field Laboratory (SW-IFL).
    • NIHHIS-funded Center for Heat Resilient Communities.
    • NOAA-funded CLIMAS (rural, border, tribal heat resilience).
    • CDC-funded BRACE (with ADHS).
    • NIH-funded Southwest Center on Resilience for Climate Change and Health (SCORCH).
  • UA Mel & Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health received $2M from CDC for AZ heat-protection interventions (2022 announcement).
  • AZ Climate & Health (UA): https://azclimatehealth.arizona.edu/resources/patients — patient-facing material.
  • UA Center for Rural Health — AZ Cooling Centers Map: https://azhealthtxt.arizona.edu/resources/az-cooling-centers-map

Arizona State University

  • Knowledge Exchange for Resilience — co-leads the Arizona Heat Resilience Workgroup; informed the Governor’s Extreme Heat Preparedness Plan. https://resilience.asu.edu/
  • Healthy Urban Environments (previously housed in the Julie Ann Wrigley Global Futures Laboratory) — heat-mitigation R&D incubator. Operations have wound down but related work continues in other Global Futures units.
  • Dr. David Hondula — ASU School of Geographical Sciences and Urban Planning; concurrent director of the City of Phoenix OHRM.

Northern Arizona University

  • Heat-related research at the Center for Health Equity Research; collaborates with NEC and ITCA-TEC on tribal climate-health work.

Participatory and public-facing tools

211 Arizona

  • Heat Relief landing: https://211arizona.org/crisis/heat-relief/
  • Utility assistance: https://211arizona.org/crisis/heat-relief/heat-relief-utility-assistance/
  • What it does. Live operators in English and Spanish help callers find cooling centers, transportation to centers, utility-bill assistance, weatherization, and emergency AC-repair referrals. Available year-round; expanded summer hours during heat season.

MAG Heat Relief Network (interactive map)

  • https://hrn.azmag.gov/ — already covered above; the public-facing flagship participatory channel.

ADHS Heat Preparedness Network (interactive map)

  • https://experience.arcgis.com/experience/c5bdf9ab90894e1baa5860c450dedb3b — statewide companion to MAG’s metro-focused map.

Clear Channel Outdoor + Maricopa County billboard partnership

  • https://www.maricopa.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/3662
  • Digital-billboard donations display heat-relief resources during the season — a model of in-kind partnership amplification.

Community Health Workers (CHWs)

  • The World Café Q4 Heat card identified CHW in-home heat-risk assessments as one of the most successful direct-engagement tactics. CHWs are deployed through county HHS, FQHCs, ITCA-TEC, and community-based organizations.

Western Regional Public Health — Train-the-Trainer (HHS Region 9)

  • 12-year-old program identified in the World Café Q4 Heat card as a proven scale channel.
