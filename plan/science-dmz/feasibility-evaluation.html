01What verification overturned

Adversarial fact-checking corrected six load-bearing assumptions in the concept. These corrections are baked into every number below and should be fixed in the concept document before external use.

  • "LoboNet at BigByte" is not UNM fiber — refuted. The "LoboNet" on BigByte's carrier roster is Lobo Internet Services Ltd. (lobo.net, AS11996), a commercial Albuquerque ISP/WISP founded 1996. No evidence of UNM-owned strands at 123 Central Ave NW was found. The 2×100G dual-VRF metro span must be budgeted as a new dark-fiber lease (≈$500–2,500/mo per pair + $2–10K NRC, 14–22 weeks provisioning; UPN, Zayo, and Lumen are on-net candidates).
  • "Rio Grande GigaPoP" does not exist. The facility is the Albuquerque GigaPoP (ABQG), operated by UNM IT itself, with downtown presence at/near the H5 carrier hotel at 505 Marquette — 3–5 blocks from BigByte. Internet2's next-gen 400G node in Albuquerque sits upstream.
  • UNM's WAN is already 100G — the bottleneck was mislocated. UNM has existing 100G links to ESnet and the Western Regional Network via ABQG. The actual constraint is CARC's ~10G campus Science-DMZ segment (2014 CC*IIE vintage). The campus-side 100G upgrade must be an explicit costed work package or the "HPC reads S3 at line rate" premise fails.
  • Education discounts are 16–35%, not 40–60%. Arista's own Texas DIR cooperative-contract sheet discloses 16.00% off list. Deeper discounts require negotiated deal registration and cannot be assumed in a grant budget. This alone raises the credible capex floor by ~$125K.
  • The $10K/yr OSN fee is unverified — it appears in no public OSN document (checked twice independently). Pod usable capacity is ~1.4 PB (not 1.5 PB), 7U, 4.1 kW, with a 6-month deactivation-notice clause in OSN's hosting guidelines. Get the fee and host obligations in writing.
  • There is no open NSF CC* solicitation as of July 2026. NSF 24-530's deadlines passed in 2024; June-2026 award announcements trace to the FY2024 cycle. The named funding vehicle currently has no submission window — the single largest external schedule risk.
  • BigByte's resilience claims are self-declared. "Tier III-compliant" carries no Uptime Institute certification; public listings describe a single 8,000-gallon generator; the earlier "2N+1 UPS" claim is unverified (the concept itself says N+1). SOC 2 Type 2 report and one-line power diagrams are required diligence before contract.

Funding-fit reality check. Both capex ends bracket the NSF CC* Area-1 ceiling ($700K/2 yr, verified from NSF 24-530). Even stripping the HSC tier (firewalls, MACsec licenses) leaves ≈$790K+ at worst-case 16%-off pricing — phasing has not yet been demonstrated numerically. The realistic funding architecture is a stack: CC* successor solicitation for the open-DMZ core, HSC institutional funds for the HIPAA tier (held outside the grant budget — 24-530 prohibits voluntary cost sharing), RIO-NM EPSCoR ($8M, UNM-led, has a Cyberinfrastructure Core) as bridge/partner, and a signed multi-year institutional O&M commitment. A regional framing with NMSU/NM Tech would unlock the $1.4M ceiling and strengthen the novel colo-siting argument.

Context rows, not UNM payroll: CyVerse RSE team (2 hired FTE + 4–6 grant-funded) operates the K8s cluster — soft-money continuity risk; a lapse strands an orphaned tenant in UNM's footprint. OSN DevOps operates the Ceph pod remotely (~0.1 FTE equivalent; UNM's obligation is the ops-technician row).
Coverage gaps the plan must own honestly.
  • The three open roles are not sufficient. Two genuinely new hires are required: the CARC Senior Network Engineer and the HSC secure-enclave engineer. Incremental loaded cost of the new hires: ≈ $212–301K/yr.
  • 24/7 is mathematically unstaffable. Google SRE on-call math needs ≥8 engineers in rotation; this model fields ~2.6 open-side FTE across 4–5 people. Declare a business-hours SLO from day one — a Friday-evening optic failure on a national-facing OSN endpoint can strand other institutions' transfers for 60+ hours, and that must be a stated, accepted service tier, not a surprise.
  • Bus factor 1 on border BGP + MACsec rollover, and on Globus/DTN internals. Cross-training and EPOC are mitigations, not depth.
  • Recruiting is the single most likely failure mode: UNM's classified band is 20–45% under ABQ market for the network engineer. Secure the reclass/market exception before the grant is submitted.
  • Security monitoring is thin for a no-firewall design: 1.0 FTE split across two orgs covers business-hours Zeek triage only. Route alerts into Central IT's 24/7 SOC even if response is next-business-day.
  • HSC compliance is under-water from 2027: the NPRM's scan/pen-test/inventory cadences roughly double the 0.25 FTE; the step-up and external pen-test procurement are acknowledged nowhere in any budget.
  • Soft-money dependencies: EPOC (offsets ~0.25 FTE of tuning) and CyVerse's 4–6 grant RSEs are both grant-contingent; the colo agreement should define the orphaned-tenant failure mode.

05SWOT

Strengths

  • Proven ESnet + Medical Science DMZ patternsCanonical no-inline-firewall design (100+ universities) and the peer-reviewed HIPAA VRF pattern in production at Indiana, Harvard, U Chicago — citable precedent, not a novel security argument.
  • UNM already operates the GigaPoP and 100G WANABQG is UNM IT's own facility; 100G to ESnet/WRN exists; Internet2's 400G node is upstream. The 2×100G handoff is incremental, not a leap.
  • Repeat CC* awardee~$498K CC*IIE (2014, built the first DMZ, used for Cancer Center genomics), $150K CC*DNI planning, ~$599K SAMPRA.
  • Operational load offloaded to OSN and CyVerseOSN runs the pod remotely (~0.1 FTE host duty); CyVerse brings its own RSE team. CARC runs only the network layer.
  • HSC cost-share with clean VRF separationThe MACsec VRF + enclave design is compliant-by-architecture with the 2027-era NPRM requirements, not just current law.
  • Colo tier fits the footprint15,000 sqft raised floor / 3 MW easily covers 2–4 racks + a 7U pod at ~$2–5K/mo — cheaper than building campus power/cooling. (Resilience claims still need diligence — see R-05.)

Weaknesses

  • The LoboNet fiber premise was wrongThe metro span must be leased new — cost, lead time, and the "flat-rate UNM fiber" story all reset.
  • Staffing can't deliver the implied service level3 open roles vs ~3.85 FTE steady-state need incl. 2 new hires; pay bands 20–45% under market; 24/7 needs ≥8 in rotation.
  • Capex likely exceeds the CC* ceiling$614K–$1.13M vs $700K, at verified 16–35% discounts; phasing asserted but not yet demonstrated numerically.
  • Concept document contains verified errorsGigaPoP name, LoboNet, WAN speed, pod capacity, OSN fee — each fixable, collectively a credibility risk in front of reviewers.

06Verdict, conditions, and critical path

Feasible with conditions. The engineering is sound and thoroughly precedented — nothing discovered makes it infeasible. But three verified findings gate everything: (1) funding — corrected capex straddles the $700K CC* ceiling and there is currently no solicitation to apply to, so the plan only closes as a stack (CC* + HSC institutional funds + RIO-NM + signed O&M commitment); (2) fiber — the free-UNM-fiber premise is refuted and the real bottleneck is CARC's own 10G campus segment; (3) staffing — the three open roles cannot deliver the stated service; ~3.85 steady-state FTE including two new hires, with a pay-band fix, and an honest business-hours SLO. If the top three conditions can't be satisfied, re-scope (campus or H5 siting, regional framing at $1.4M) rather than push forward on current assumptions.

Go/no-go conditions (priority order)

  1. Secure the funding stack first: program-officer conversation on CC* successor timing; HSC institutional funding of the HIPAA tier held outside the grant; RIO-NM alignment. Do not start procurement on a single-source $700K plan the corrected capex cannot fit.
  2. Resolve the metro fiber question in writing within 30 days; absent UNM strands, budget the commercial dark-fiber lease and start its RFP immediately on award.
  3. Signed multi-year CIO + HSC O&M and staffing commitment covering the reconciled recurring figure (~$315–615K/yr incl. net-new staff) in the mandatory Campus CI Plan.
  4. Fix staffing before submission: reclass/market-exception the network engineer; commit both new hires; convert the 50/50 split into a signed MOU; declare the business-hours SLO.
  5. Make the CARC campus-side 100G upgrade an explicit costed work package.
  6. Gate all PHI work on: executed BAA, reviewed SOC 2 Type 2 + power diligence, formal hybrid-entity designation, written CISO + HSC ISO sign-off on the compensating-controls analysis.
  7. Re-baseline at 16–35% off list; firm quotes for all quote-only items; OSN fee confirmed in writing.
  8. Harden the BigByte contract against small-vendor risk (disclosure, notice, assignment, SLAs, H5 relocation path).
  9. Execute the three-party operating MOU before any equipment is ordered.
  10. Correct the concept document's verified errors (ABQG, fiber, 100G WAN, 1.4 PB) before external use.

Kill criteria

  • BigByte refuses the BAA, fails SOC 2/power diligence, or shows viability warning signs → kill the HSC cage at BigByte even if the open DMZ proceeds.

07Method & caveats

20 agents, 540 tool calls: six research sweeps with web access → one adversarial fact-checker per sweep (independent re-verification of the 5–8 most load-bearing figures; verdicts of refuted override the original claim) → four parallel synthesis agents (risk register, SWOT, cost, staffing) → an integrating feasibility verdict → a completeness critic that found 16 gaps (2 critical, both researched and folded in above; 11 important, listed as flags and unpriced items; 3 minor).

Caveats. Quote-only items (capture NICs, taps/broker, BigByte rack rates, ABQG cost-shares, MACsec licenses) are derived estimates flagged in-line. The staffing model's build-phase total has a ~0.45 FTE internal inconsistency (4.8 vs 5.25) to reconcile. The Central-IT half of the security split may be double-counted vs the concept's single split role. HSC's ~40% fringe rate needs verification against the HSC FY26 memo. Enclave firewalls (10–40G) vs the 100G VRF is an unacknowledged sizing decision needing an HSC workload analysis. All figures 2026 USD, no out-year escalation.
