System Design — Unusual Animal Health Events as Early Warning

A completed instance of the design worksheet, focused on capturing unusual animal health events as an early warning signal for human public health.

1. Purpose of the system

  • Capture animal health events.
  • Capture and track unusual animal health events as an early warning sign for public health.

2. Target population

  • Animal owners.
  • Caretakers.

3. What will you give back? (bi-directionality)

  • Education (audio-visual, text).
  • Prevention of disease transmission.
  • Resources.
  • Allocation / escalating reports to the right agencies.
  • Platform of reporting.
  • Access to veterinary resources.
  • Pet food / other basic resources.
  • Rebate / discounted diagnostics.
  • Vet consultation.

4. Parameter changes vs. the Minimum Dataset

Keep / emphasize:

  • Clinical / behavioral events.
  • Date of onset.
  • Vet consultations.
  • What has been done.
  • Travel history.
  • Animal residence / setting.
  • Animal interactions with humans / other animals.
  • Auxiliary information (photos / videos).

Add:

  • Lineage.
  • Demographics.
  • Geographic ZIP.
  • Contact with tick.
  • Health history.
  • Wildlife bites.
  • Type of animal.
  • Other animals in home / environment.

5. Ensuring access (barriers, literacy, awareness)

  • Network with governments, animal control, shelters, and vets.
  • Public buildings (libraries, food stores).
  • Easy user interface and interactions (collaboration with existing tech).
  • Schools.
  • Computer-based platform.
  • App (iPhone / Android) with multi-lingual support.
  • Work with local vets and animal shelters (spay/neuter, low-cost vet clinics).
  • Address the trust barrier.

6. Frequency of reporting

  • “As often as you see something, say (report) something.”
  • Report without restriction — all reports go through a PH filtering system for action.

7. Time per report

  • ~10 minutes or less.

8. Key partners

  • Pet stores.
  • Tech team.
  • Government partners.
  • 4-H.
  • RANS.
  • Animal shelters.
  • Public health (state and local).
  • Academic partners.
  • Cooperative extension.
  • Veterinarians.
  • Rescue groups.
  • Vet specialists.
  • Schools.

9. Validation / verification

  • Deploy first to populations like animal control, veterinarians, and veterinary students.
  • Filter by IP address, ZIP code, frequency of reporting a particular event, veterinary follow-up, diagnostic follow-up, symptom cluster.

10. Elevator pitch

We’re gathering a multi-disciplinary team of human and animal health professionals to develop a platform that captures and tracks unusual animal health events. This can serve as an early warning sign for disease or health events in people. This platform should be targeted toward animal owners and caretakers, where they can report varying kinds of information about their animals. Education and veterinary resources would be provided back to animal caretakers, and data would be used to identify clustered events that trigger public health or veterinary action.

Source: Ending Pandemics Academy · University of Arizona Mel & Enid Zuckerman College of Public Health · Global Health Institute.
